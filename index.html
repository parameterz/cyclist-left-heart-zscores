How the Z-score works
Z = k · ln(y / μ)

k is just 1/σ on the log scale

This is the familiar (value − mean) / SD, applied on the log scale: Z = (ln y − ln μ) / σ, so k = 1/σ. Here y is the measured value and μ the predicted value. Because the SD is proportional to the predicted size, one σ fits every athlete, and each Z corresponds to a fixed percent of predicted.

μ = C · Heighta · (male factor) · (endurance factor) · RRb, with height in metres and RR in seconds.

The paper maps ln(% predicted) to Gaussian Z with an optimized piecewise-linear function. This mockup uses the single published slope; Table 5 percentiles agree with it to within about 0.1 Z.

Open questions for the authors
  1. LV stroke volume: RR0.27 (Table 4) or no RR term (Table S3)?
  2. Cardiac output: constant 1.89 (Table 4) or 1.81 (Table S3)? Was an RR term tested? Validation mean Z was −0.59.
  3. LV mass: RR exponent 0.07 (Table 4) or 0.1 (Table S3)?
  4. LV diastolic diameter: endurance factor 1.02 (Table S3) or none (Table 4)? At Table 1 cohort means both versions predict about 4% below the observed diameters.
  5. LV systolic diameter: confirm Table 4 (24.02 · Ht0.6 · 1.04 male, k = 10.57). The Table S3 row repeats the LVEDV terms.
  6. Derivation cohort heart rate (mean, SD, range) and height range, for out-of-range warnings.
  7. Should the piecewise-linear Gaussian mapping be implemented, and if so, what are its knots?
  8. A few anonymized cases with expected Z-scores, to use as test cases.

Derivation cohort: 181 elite British cyclists (105 male, 76 female; 128 endurance, 53 sprint), age 22.7 ± 5.5 years. External validation: 62 elite male endurance cyclists. Measurements: biplane Simpson LV volumes, biplane LA volume, ASE corrected linear LV mass, 2D parasternal long-axis LV diameters. Equations may not apply to other populations, lower training volumes, or different measurement methods.
